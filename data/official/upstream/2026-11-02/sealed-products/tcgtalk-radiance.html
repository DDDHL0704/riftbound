What Is Riftbound Radiance?

Riftbound Radiance is the fifth set for the Riftbound TCG, Riot's physical card game set in the League of Legends universe. It lands October 23, 2026 worldwide, with Pre-Rift prerelease events running October 16–22. Radiance is the music-and-performance set — Seraphine headlines it, the alt-art Runes carry music-themed art, and the set's Ultimate rarity is a concert venue.

The set contains 180 cards including 66 Showcase treatments per Riot's own materials (community trackers count 167 collectible, a counting-convention difference on Showcase variants rather than a conflict). It introduces three new keywords — Deploy, Show Off and Disarm — plus Equipment Units and Bomb tokens, all covered in our Radiance mechanics guide.

Radiance carries nine Legends — Seraphine, Evelynn, Ekko, Ziggs, Jarvan IV, Orianna, K'Sante, Mordekaiser and Riven — each with an Overnumbered variant and an artist-signed Overnumbered on top of that, so the Legend chase is nine cards deep across two premium treatments. Sitting above all of it is Packed Amphitheater, Riftbound's second-ever Ultimate rarity card after Baron Nashor in Unleashed.

Box Contents & Pack Structure

Radiance is available in the following retail formats:

  • Booster Display (Box) — 24 packs per display, the same as Unleashed and Vendetta
  • Radiance Vault Bundle — 6 packs, a storage box, full-art foil tokens (gold and Bomb) and a complete set of 36 basic Runes
  • Seraphine vs Evelynn Showdown Decks — two 56-card preconstructed decks, two Radiance packs, two playmats and deckboxes, $34.99 MSRP
  • Pre-Rift Event Kits — store-allocated: a 15-card mini-precon, five packs and the Heimerdinger, Academy Instructor promo

Each booster pack contains three foil cards: one foil Common or Uncommon, and two foil Rare-or-above slots. Those two rare+ slots are where Legends, Epics, Showcase alt arts, Overnumbered and the Ultimate can appear. The rest of the pack is non-foil Commons and Uncommons.

Not every pack yields an Epic. With two rare+ slots per pack, plenty of packs come out as a Legend plus a regular Rare — technically two rare-or-above cards, practically a blank. In the first Radiance box we have a complete card-by-card tally for, at most 12 of the 24 packs contained an Epic or better.

Booster display basics

  • 24 packs per booster display
  • 48 total rare+ slots per display (2 per pack × 24 packs)
  • MSRP approximately $120 USD / €120, same as Vendetta
  • Note that the Vault's 36 Runes are the basic Rune set — Radiance's music-themed alt-art Runes come from packs, roughly one per two displays

Pull Rate Data: 330 Packs

The data below is aggregated from community openings totalling 330 packs — thirteen 24-pack booster displays (312 packs) plus three 6-pack Vault Bundles (18 packs). Seven displays are promotional Radiance Summit product from seven creators; the other six are one complete sealed case broken on October 22 by openers with no disclosed Riot relationship. Retail Pre-Rift kit openings are covered in their own section and are in neither count.

⚠️ The tiers no longer share a denominator, and you should know why before reading the table. The sealed case is a two-opener pack battle. It calls Overnumbered, Ultimate and signature cards clearly, but it uses "alternate" loosely and counts basic Runes alongside Showcase Runes, so no Showcase or Rune numerator can be recovered from it. Overnumbered and Ultimate therefore rest on all 330 packs; Showcase and Rune still rest on the 168 promotional display packs. Each row says which. We would rather carry two denominators than quietly divide one tier's count by another tier's packs.

Updated October 19 — and this is the second time the Showcase figure has moved. We published this page on two displays, revised it on four, and have now revised it again on seven. Both revisions hit the same tier for the same reason: a pattern that held across every box we had, then broke when more boxes arrived. It is worth being blunt about that, because it is the most useful thing a small sample can teach you.

Three caveats you should read before the main table. First, every box here is promotional product supplied by Riot to creators at the Radiance Summit — and one of the seven openers discloses working at Riot Games. Creator product can run hot, so read the hit tiers as a best case. Second, only two of the seven displays were tallied card by card; the rest were commentary openings that skipped most Rares, so they pin down the Showcase, Rune, Overnumbered and Ultimate tiers but not the Epic or Legend counts. Third, one display produced the Ultimate and two Overnumbered on its own while the other six produced no Overnumbered at all — its opener says Riot juiced it, and we think he is probably right. It is pooled into the rates anyway, but read that section before you lean on the chase-tier numbers.

The other thing this update did was close the gap with previous sets. At 54 and 102 packs Radiance looked markedly hotter than Vendetta or Unleashed. At 330 packs it does not. Alt-art Showcase Epics measure 2.29 per display against Vendetta's 2.21 — a real count over 168 packs, and close to exact. Overnumbered has since gone past the anchor and out the other side: a sealed case took it to 0.29 per display against Unleashed's 0.41. Epics are weaker evidence — a 6–8 observed range around a midpoint of 7, which brackets the cross-set 6–7 rather than landing on it — but it points the same way. The early "Radiance runs hot" story looks like small-sample noise, and the working read is that Radiance opens like a normal Riftbound box.

Read the Total column first. Four, four, three, three, four, three, four. Every display in the sample delivered three or four Showcase-treatment cards and no display delivered five or two. That is the finding to plan around, and it is the only Showcase claim on this page that has survived every expansion of the sample.

Now read across the rows, because the columns are where it gets interesting. Display 6 looks like a disaster on the alt-art Epic column — one, against three in each of the five boxes before it — until you notice it pulled two alt-art Runes, which no other box did. Display 7 looks worse still at zero alt-art Epics, until you notice it pulled two Overnumbered and the Ultimate. Neither box was short-changed. They both got their three-or-four; the cards just landed on different rungs of the ladder.

That is what you would expect from a fixed slot budget. Each pack carries two rare-or-above slots, 48 per display, and the premium treatments compete for the same handful of them. An Overnumbered does not come on top of your alt arts — it comes instead of one. Which means the box that hits the chase card will usually look thin everywhere else, and a box with three alt-art Epics and a Rune is not a lucky box, it is the ordinary one.

The corollary is worth saying plainly: the community was right and we were wrong. Openers on three separate videos said on camera that they believed a Radiance box held two alt arts and a Rune. We told you it was three alt arts guaranteed. Seven boxes in, the measured figures are 2.29 alt-art Epics and 0.86 Runes per display — which is, almost exactly, two alt arts and a Rune.

One structural detail before you chase a specific Showcase: the alt arts are drawn from several League skinlines rather than one. Display 3 pulled three K/DA cards (Kai'Sa, Seraphine, Evelynn); displays 4 and 5 pulled Pool Party treatments of Jarvan IV and Seraphine.

And the Heartsteel cards are a different animal entirely. Several openers said they believed Heartsteel art was scarcer than K/DA; we originally logged that as word of mouth. Riot's published checklist shows why they were right, and it is more structural than a rarity difference. The five Heartsteel cards — Aphelios Exalted (RAD-179), Ezreal Dashing (180), Yone Blademaster (181), Kayn Unleashed (182) and Sett Kingpin (183) — sit inside the Overnumbered range and have no base-set printing at all. They are not a flavour of Showcase you can open at Showcase rates. The only way a Heartsteel card comes out of a pack is as an Overnumbered, a tier six of our seven displays never produced once. If you want Sett or Kayn, buy the single.

The Box That Hit Everything

Display 7 deserves its own section, because it is simultaneously the most exciting box in this dataset and the least trustworthy.

In 24 packs it produced:

  • The Packed Amphitheater Ultimate — the first confirmed pull of the card anywhere, at a Riot-stated 1 in 4,000 packs
  • An Overnumbered Mordekaiser
  • An Overnumbered Ziggs, numbered RAD 169, with every Ziggs bomb from every Ziggs skin worked into the border art
  • One alt-art Rune — and zero plain alt-art Epics

The other six displays in this sample produced no Overnumbered at all between them, across 144 packs. One box holding the Ultimate and two Overnumbered while six boxes hold none is not how variance usually looks.

Nor is it a conclusion we had to reach on our own. Mid-opening, before the Ziggs appeared, the creator says it himself: "Riot knows I bring the people to Riftbound. They're like, yo, we're going to juice your box, right? Like they know me, right?" He was joking. The box then produced two more premium cards.

Run the numbers and the joke stops being funny. At Riot's stated 0.025%, the expected number of Ultimates in 168 display packs is 0.04. Seeing one is about a 4% outcome on its own. One Ultimate in 330 packs would imply a rate of 0.30% per pack — roughly 12 times what Riot published for the print run. Stack that on top of eleven other displays with zero Overnumbered, including six consecutive boxes of a sealed case, and the simplest explanation is that this box was not a random box.

So what did we do with it? We left it in. It is pooled into every rate on this page, because dropping a box because its result is inconvenient is a worse sin than keeping one we are suspicious of — and because we cannot prove it was seeded. What we have not done is let it set the Ultimate rate. That row still reads Riot's 0.025%, and our calculator will not re-derive it from this sample. If you are buying retail product on October 23, plan on 1 in 4,000 packs, not on what happened to one creator eleven days early.

The same display's Vault, for what it is worth, was the worst in the sample: its owner called it "giga low rolled" and got one Epic from six packs. Even a juiced box does not win everywhere.

What a Sealed Case Yields

On October 22, a day before launch, two openers broke a complete six-box case — 144 packs — as a pack battle. It is the first case-level Radiance data anywhere, and it is the first substantial product in this page's sample that did not come from Riot: there is no thank-you to Riot, no sponsorship disclosure and no Summit mention, in contrast to all seven promotional displays, every one of which opens by crediting Riot. They also break cases for previous Riftbound sets as a matter of routine.

That case produced, across 144 packs:

  • One Overnumbered, in box 2. Boxes 5 and 6 explicitly went looking for a second and confirmed there wasn't one.
  • Zero Ultimate. Zero signed Overnumbered.
  • Five signature cards — Demacian Standard (Jarvan IV), Ntofo Strikes (K'Sante), Last Caress (Evelynn), The Ball (Orianna) and Blade of the Exile (Riven).
  • An Aphelios Exalted (RAD-179) in box 4 — one of the five Heartsteel cards that live only in the Overnumbered range. The openers called it "the K/DA alternate" and said outright they did not know what K/DA stood for, so read the card name rather than their label.
  • Box 2 counted "seven epics" aloud mid-box, which corroborates the 6–8 Epic range from a box nobody was given for free. It is a floor — the count came before the box finished.

Their own summary of a box: "nine hits in a box, about, like everything epic and up." Their summary of the case: "completely underwhelming."

The useful thing a case tells you

A case does not buy you a chase card. Six consecutive sealed boxes, 144 packs, $720 of product at MSRP, produced one Overnumbered between them and nothing above it. If you are buying a case expecting the volume to guarantee a top-tier hit, this is the data point to weigh.

And it ran colder than the promotional boxes. That is the single most useful thing in this update. Every hit rate on this page has carried a "creator boxes run hot" warning since October 12, and until now there was nothing to test it against. At the rate we published before the case, 144 packs should have yielded about 2.3 Overnumbered. It yielded one. That is the direction the caveat predicts, and it is why the Overnumbered figure on this page came down from ~0.4 per display to ~0.3.

What this case cannot tell you

It cannot test the Showcase finding, and we are not going to pretend otherwise. A pack battle is two people calling cards fast across a table. These openers use "alternate" for anything with a different treatment, and in the wrap-up they sort basic Runes in with the Showcase Runes — "these are all the runes, these ugly runes, you got so many". There is no honest way to extract a Showcase or alt-art Rune numerator from that, so those two tiers still rest on the 168 promotional display packs and the 3–4-treatments-per-display finding still stands on seven boxes, not thirteen.

It also cannot settle the Ultimate. One case adding zero is exactly what you would expect at 1 in 4,000 either way — 144 packs carries about a 3.5% chance of containing one. What it does do is make the box that pulled the Ultimate look slightly more unusual than it already did.

Retail Pre-Rift Kits: The First Non-Promotional Data

Every booster box above came from Riot. That is the single biggest weakness of this page, and it is why every hit tier has carried a "creator boxes run hot" warning since October 12. As of October 19 we finally have a little data from the retail channel: two Pre-Rift event kits, bought over the counter at local game stores during the October 16–22 prerelease window.

What a Pre-Rift kit actually contains, confirmed on camera from the packaging:

  • A seeded preconstructed booster built around one of the nine Legends — fixed contents, not a random pack
  • Five ordinary booster packs
  • The Heimerdinger, Academy Instructor promo, stamped as a pre-release card — the only way to get it
  • Price paid: $35

What the two kits produced:

  • Kit one: a Jarvan IV Overnumbered from the five packs, plus Chronobreak (Ekko's signature) and an Ekko alt-art Showcase. This is the first Overnumbered anyone has recorded from non-promotional Radiance product.
  • Kit two: nothing above Epic in five packs. Zero Point Inflection was the best card in it.

Two kits is not a rate and we are not going to pretend otherwise. These openings are not pooled into the 330-pack figures: the pack counts are not clean (the first opener also ripped an unknown number of event prize packs over the course of the night) and a kit is a different product from a display. What they do give is a sanity check, and the sanity check passes — a retail-channel Overnumbered exists, and the one kit that produced nothing produced nothing in exactly the way the display data predicts.

On whether a Pre-Rift kit is worth buying: not for the packs. Kit two's opener did the arithmetic out loud — $35 for five packs plus a fixed booster is dearer per pack than a $120 display — and concluded he would not recommend it for ripping. Buy a kit for the event and for the Heimerdinger promo, which is genuinely unobtainable otherwise. Full product breakdown in our Radiance products guide.

One incidental confirmation worth recording: kit two's opener had never handled a Riftbound card before and worked out the pack structure from scratch on camera — "it looks like there's always three foils in a pack, and then the last card is a full art." An opener with no knowledge of the set independently arriving at three foils per pack is about as clean a corroboration of the structure as this kind of data gets.

Named Notable Pulls

Card names below are transcribed from the October 12–19 openings. Names that match Riot's revealed card list, or that two independent openers said the same way, are given in full; a handful are single-source reveals whose exact wording may be corrected at launch, and those are flagged.

  • The Ultimate: Packed Amphitheater — the first confirmed pull of Riftbound's second-ever Ultimate rarity, from display 7. Read the section on that box before drawing conclusions from it.
  • Every Overnumbered recorded so far: Orianna, Lady of Clockwork (from a 6-pack Vault, alongside Orianna's signature spell in the same six packs); Mordekaiser and Ziggs (both from display 7, the Ziggs numbered RAD 169 with every Ziggs bomb from every Ziggs skin in the border); Jarvan IV (from a retail Pre-Rift kit); and an Ekko from a Summit pre-Rift hand-out box. Six of the seven booster displays produced none at all.
  • Showcase Epics, display one: alt-art Seraphine, Ziggs and Riven — three champion Showcases, one of each, plus one alt-art Rune.
  • Showcase Epics, display two: an alt-art red Ekko Epic (the opener's pick of the box), alt-art Evelynn, Consuming, and an Akali Showcase in K/DA art — again three, again plus one alt-art Rune.
  • Showcase Epics, display three: Seraphine, Inspiring (RAD-065a), an Evelynn Showcase, and a Kai'Sa, Rebel (RAD-063) the opener called an alt art — three by her count, no Rune. One caveat on that third card: the published alt-art list carries exactly 18 Showcases, two champion units for each of the nine Legends, and Kai'Sa is not among them. Either the list is incomplete or what she pulled was a foil base print. We have left her count as recorded rather than quietly revising a measured box on an unofficial checklist — but if Kai'Sa has no Showcase, display three had two, not three.
  • Showcase Epics, display four: alt-art Riven, a Pool Party Jarvan IV and another Seraphine — three again, no Rune.
  • Showcase Epics, display five: a Pool Party Jarvan IV, a Pool Party Seraphine and an Orianna, plus the alt-art Mind Rune — whose owner noted on camera that this put the box "one alt art over the typical guaranteed hit rates."
  • Showcase Epics, display six: just one — an alt-art Mordekaiser Epic — but two alt-art Runes, Chaos and Calm, the only box in the sample to pull two. Its Vault separately produced a K'Sante Showcase.
  • Showcase Epics, display seven: none. Its premium slots went to the Ultimate, two Overnumbered and the alt-art Body Rune instead.
  • Signature cards pulled: Chronobreak (Ekko), The Ball (Orianna), Realm of Death (Mordekaiser — gives an enemy unit −2 might and a friendly unit +2, then has them deal damage equal to their might to each other, a 4-might swing that removes almost anything), Hexplosive Minefield (Ziggs), Ntofo Strikes (K'Sante), Demacian Standard (Jarvan IV), Encore (Seraphine), and Blade of the Exile — Riven's signature, which is a gear rather than a spell: three energy, equipped by recycling or exhausting a friendly unit, granting +3 might and Ganking. Signature cards sit at Epic and were the most common Epic type in the sample.
  • The signature structure, confirmed against the official checklist: the nine Legends occupy RAD 139–156 as champion-unit-plus-signature pairs — Ekko/Chronobreak, Ziggs/Hexplosive Minefield, Mordekaiser/Realm of Death, Orianna/The Ball, K'Sante/Ntofo Strikes, Riven/Blade of the Exile, Seraphine/Encore, Evelynn/Last Caress, Jarvan IV/Demacian Standard. Nine Legends, nine signatures, no exceptions.
  • Epics pulled: K'Sante, Dauntless, the yellow Mordekaiser Epic, the base Orianna Epic, two copies of Orianna, Lady of Clockwork, and an 8-energy purple Epic unit with built-in Deflect and no power cost.
  • Legends pulled: Mordekaiser, Iron Revenant, Evelynn, Agony's Embrace, Riven, Broken Blade, Ziggs, Short-Fused, Orianna, Mechanical Marvel, Seraphine, Starry-Eyed Songstress, Jarvan IV, Exemplar of Demacia and the K'Sante Legend — most boxes will see most of the nine.
  • Packed Amphitheater, the ordinary print: four of the seven displays named the normal non-Ultimate version of the battlefield, two of them twice. The other three never mentioned it, which given that they skipped most Rares means nothing either way. Pulling the card is routine; pulling the Ultimate treatment of it is the 1-in-4,000 event, and exactly one box in this sample managed that.
  • Battlefields and foils: Cosmic Vista came up repeatedly, including a back-to-back pair in one display. Foil battlefields turned up most packs and are the cheapest thing in the set to complete by opening.
  • Now corroborated by two or more independent openers: Firebrand Raider (6 energy, 6 might, readies all enemy units and stops the awaken-phase ready), Ephemeral Ward, Remember the Fallen, Spirit of the Forge, Titanic Shrine, Whirling Twirligig, Gutter Guardian, Hush, Cataclysm, Black Market, Supernova, Riptide Mage, Cosmic Vista, Staunch Survivor, Resource Extractor, Scorched Earth, Gravity Field, Mechanical Bandit, Hunting Blind, Angler Wyrm, Scrutinizing Scope, Portable Portal, Bushwhack Trap, Zero Point Inflection, Drehound Packmaster, Demacian Illuminators, Seraphic Wyvern, Heartening Soul, Firelights' Hideout, Akali Brash, Neeko Blending In and Rally for War. Also confirmed: Kai'Sa, Rebel is a Radiance card (RAD-063), and Ekko has two Overnumbered variants.
  • Every name above is now checked against Riot's published RAD checklist, which is worth saying because transcripts garble them badly. Five we previously printed were wrong: it is Blade of the Exile (not Blade of Exile), Whirling Twirligig (not Twirlygig), Angler Wyrm (not Angler Worm), Firelights' Hideout (not Firelight Hideout), and Akali Brash — which one opener read aloud as "Acolyte Brash".
  • A box does not contain all nine Legends. Display 6's openers checked deliberately at the end and found they were an Orianna short — they only completed the nine by counting the Legend from their Vault. Their conclusion, verbatim: "a box won't guarantee you one of each." This page previously claimed the opposite, on four boxes that happened to show all nine.

Key Findings

  • Three or four Showcase treatments per display — seven boxes out of seven. Count alt-art Epics, alt-art Runes, Overnumbered and the Ultimate together and every box in the sample landed on 3 or 4. None landed on 2 or 5. This is the figure to plan around, and it is the only Showcase claim here that has survived both expansions of the sample.
  • "Exactly three alt-art Epics" is dead, and we published it. The per-display counts are 3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 1, 0, averaging 2.29. The two low boxes are not unlucky boxes — one spent its slots on a second alt-art Rune, the other on two Overnumbered and the Ultimate. The premium slots are a fixed budget, so a chase card displaces an alt art rather than adding to it.
  • The community's number was better than ours. Openers on three separate videos said they expected two alt arts and a Rune. We said three alt arts guaranteed. The measured figures are 2.29 alt-art Epics and 0.86 Runes per display. They were right.
  • Epics look normal after all, on thin evidence. A second, near-complete display yielded at least 6 base Epics against the first one's counted 8. A 6–8 range brackets the 6–7 Vendetta and Unleashed measured, so the "Radiance runs hot on Epics" story this page ran for a week is probably just variance — but one extra box is not proof, and the 6 is a floor.
  • The hit tiers have converged on the previous sets — and Overnumbered went past them. Showcase measures 2.29 per display against Vendetta's 2.21, a real count over 168 packs that lands within a rounding error. Overnumbered started at 0.39 against Unleashed's 0.41 and then a sealed case took it to 0.29, below the anchor. Radiance opens like a normal Riftbound box, and a slightly stingy one at the chase tier — the early data just did not have enough boxes in it to say so.
  • Eleven of thirteen displays had zero Overnumbered. Plan for none. The pooled rate of ~0.3 per display is carried by a Vault, one suspect box, and a single copy from a six-box case. A display with no Overnumbered is the ordinary outcome.
  • A sealed case does not buy you a chase card. Six consecutive boxes, 144 packs, ~$720 at MSRP — one Overnumbered between them and nothing above it. Its openers called the case "completely underwhelming".
  • The creator-box caveat finally got tested, and it held. That case is the first substantial product here that did not come from Riot, and it ran colder than the promotional boxes — one Overnumbered where the previous rate predicted about 2.3. That is why this page's Overnumbered figure came down rather than up.
  • The Ultimate was pulled — in the one box we do not trust. First confirmed Packed Amphitheater, from the same display that produced two Overnumbered and whose opener jokes that Riot juiced it. We report it and refuse to re-derive the rate from it: that would imply ~22x Riot's published figure off a single observation. Full reasoning here.
  • A box does not guarantee all nine Legends. One set of openers checked and came up an Orianna short, completing the nine only with their Vault. We claimed the opposite last week on four boxes that happened to show all nine.
  • First retail data exists, and it behaves. Two over-the-counter Pre-Rift kits: one produced a Jarvan IV Overnumbered in five packs, the other nothing above Epic. Not a rate, but the first evidence from outside Riot's own boxes.
  • Creator-box caveat, still. Every display here came from the Radiance Summit, and one opener discloses working at Riot Games. Read the hit tiers as a ceiling until retail displays are counted after October 23.
  • The Ultimate is not a plan. Riot's stated 0.025% puts Packed Amphitheater at 1 in 4,000 packs, 1 per 167 displays, 1 per 28 sealed cases — roughly $20,000 of boxes per copy at MSRP.
  • Roughly half of packs are blanks. At least 12 of 24 packs in the fully tallied display contained nothing above a Legend or regular Rare — and that is a floor, since several Epics shared packs.

Should You Open or Buy Singles?

Open if:

  • You want the Showcase cards — every one of the seven displays we have measured delivered three or four Showcase treatments, so the count is about as predictable as sealed product gets even though the tier split is not, and Radiance is the first Riftbound set with newly commissioned art for them
  • You want playsets of Commons and Uncommons for competitive play — Radiance's best cards (The Good Stuff, Portable Portal, Trifarian Captain) are low-rarity, and opening is the fastest route to a pile of them
  • You are building toward a master set and enjoy the opening experience for its own sake
  • You collect foil battlefields — they appear in most packs and are the cheapest completion target in the set

Buy singles if:

  • You want Packed Amphitheater. 1 in 4,000 packs. There is no version of opening that makes sense here
  • You want a specific Overnumbered. Eleven of our thirteen displays produced zero, and a whole sealed case produced one. Across a 16-card pool, with signed versions on top, hitting the one you want is a lottery
  • You want a specific signature card. Nine Legends, nine signatures, and only ~7 Epics per box spread across the whole Epic pool — buy the signature for the Legend you actually play
  • You want to play the deck. The strongest cards in Radiance sit at Common and Uncommon. A full competitive playset of them will cost less than a single display

The numbers on chasing Packed Amphitheater

  • Riot's stated rate is 0.025% of packs — 1 in 4,000 packs
  • At 24 packs per display that is 1 per 167 displays, or 1 per 28 sealed cases
  • At $120 MSRP per display, the expected spend for one copy is north of $20,000 — and that is the expectation, not a cap; half of all attempts cost more
  • Our 330-pack sample holds one — but at the stated rate 330 packs is only about an 8% chance of seeing one, and it landed in the single box we have reason to think was seeded. A full sealed case opened afterwards added none. One hit in 330 packs would imply ~12× the published rate, which is why we have not changed the number
  • Even at a four-figure secondary price, buying the single is an order of magnitude cheaper than the expected cost of opening for it

The numbers on chasing a specific Overnumbered

  • Plan on roughly 1 Overnumbered per 3 displays on the pooled rate — now slightly below Unleashed's measured ~1.7% per pack, after a sealed case produced one in 144
  • But weight that against the distribution: six of our seven displays produced zero across 144 packs, and both display-sourced Overnumbered came from the same suspect box. A box with none is the ordinary experience
  • The Overnumbered pool is exactly 16 cards (RAD 168–183), confirmed against Riot's checklist, so a named Overnumbered is about a sixteenth as likely as "any" Overnumbered
  • That puts a specific unsigned Overnumbered at roughly 1 per 55 displays — about $6,600 of boxes at MSRP, against an expected single price under $120
  • The signed version is rarer again, and none has appeared in 330 packs, a full sealed case included

The honest bottom line: at 330 packs, Radiance opens like a normal Riftbound box, and slightly worse than that at the top. For a week this page told you otherwise — hotter on Epics, three guaranteed alt arts, fewer Legends. More boxes took almost all of it back. Alt-art Showcase Epics are 2.3 per box, not 3. Epics look like 6–8 rather than a clean 8, which brackets the previous sets. And a sealed case has now pushed Overnumbered below Unleashed, to about one per three displays. What survives is narrower but genuinely useful: every display delivers three or four Showcase-treatment cards, and which rung of the ladder they land on is the part you cannot control. Eleven of thirteen boxes produced no Overnumbered at all, and a whole $720 case produced one. Half your packs are still a Legend and a Rare. The Ultimate has been pulled once, by a creator whose box we think was seeded, and it is still a $20,000 expected spend for anyone else. The cards that win games are Uncommons you can buy for pocket change. Open Radiance for the two-or-three alt arts and the Rune, which are lovely in person and the most likely thing in the set to be underpriced in launch week. Buy singles for anything you actually want. And note what the one non-promotional case did to these numbers: it moved them down. Retail openings start in volume from October 23, and on this page's record you should expect them to move something again.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does every Riftbound Radiance pack guarantee an Epic?

No. Every pack has three foils — one foil Common or Uncommon and two rare-or-above slots — but those two slots are frequently filled by Legends (at Rare) and regular Rares rather than Epics. In the first of our seven displays, the one with a complete card-by-card tally, at most half the packs contained an Epic or better. The two rare+ slots are where the higher rarities can appear, not where they always do. An opener who bought a retail Pre-Rift kit and had never handled a Riftbound card worked the structure out from scratch on camera and reached the same conclusion.

How many Epics do you get per Riftbound Radiance booster box?

Roughly 7 base Epics plus 2–3 alt-art Showcase Epics — around 9 Epic-rarity cards per box. Be aware how thin this is: exactly one of our seven displays got a complete card-by-card tally, and it produced 8 base Epics. A second display was opened near-completely with rarities flagged as they came, yielding at least 6. So the honest statement is a range of 6–8 around a midpoint of 7, which brackets the 6–7 per box that Vendetta and Unleashed each measured over far bigger samples rather than confirming it. This page originally reported 8 and said Radiance ran hot on Epics; a second box is enough to make that look like variance, not enough to prove the opposite. Read the 6 as a floor — that opener said she was moving fast through the commons, and two of her six were signature cards we classify as Epic by convention. The remaining five displays never labelled rarities at all.

What is the Packed Amphitheater pull rate in Radiance?

Riot has stated the Ultimate rarity appears in roughly 0.025% of booster packs across the print run — about 1 in 4,000 packs, 1 per 167 booster displays, or 1 per 28 sealed cases. Our 330-pack sample holds one — the only confirmed Packed Amphitheater Ultimate anywhere. We have not changed the rate because of it. It came from the one display that also produced two Overnumbered while eleven other displays produced none, and whose opener says on camera that Riot juiced his box; one hit in 330 packs would imply roughly 12× the published figure off that single observation, and a complete sealed case opened afterwards added none. At the stated rate, 330 packs is about an 8% shot, so a lone hit is unremarkable — if you are buying retail, plan on 1 in 4,000. Note separately that several displays pulled the ordinary non-Ultimate print of the battlefield, and the case openers spent their wrap-up explaining the difference while holding that ordinary print — it is a routine Rare.

What is the Overnumbered pull rate in Radiance?

Four in 330 packs — about 0.3 per display, or one per three boxes, which now sits slightly below the ~1.7% per pack Unleashed measured. The distribution matters much more than the average: eleven of our thirteen displays produced zero. The four are an Orianna, Lady of Clockwork from a 6-pack Vault, a Mordekaiser and a Ziggs (RAD-169) from the single display we suspect was seeded, and one from box 2 of a sealed six-box case. That case is why this number came down: 144 non-promotional packs produced one copy where the previous rate predicted about 2.3. So the pooled rate is the right planning number, but a box with none is the ordinary experience, not bad luck. Two further Overnumbered sit outside the sample on purpose: a Jarvan IV from a retail Pre-Rift kit (real channel, but no clean pack count) and an Ekko from a Summit pre-Rift hand-out box (curated product, not sealed packs).

How many alt-art Showcase cards are in a Radiance box?

Three or four Showcase treatments — in all seven displays, with no exceptions. That is the number to rely on, and it means every tier counted together: alt-art Epics, alt-art Runes, Overnumbered and the Ultimate. The split between those tiers is not reliable at all. Alt-art Epics ran 3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 1, 0 across the seven boxes (mean 2.29) and alt-art Runes ran 1, 1, 0, 0, 1, 2, 1 (mean 0.86). The box with zero alt-art Epics is the box that pulled two Overnumbered and the Ultimate; the box with only one pulled two Runes. Premium slots are a fixed budget, so a chase card displaces a plain alt art rather than adding to it. This page previously told you three alt-art Epics were effectively guaranteed, on four boxes that happened to deliver exactly three — the community's own estimate of "two alt arts and a Rune", repeated on three separate openings, turned out closer to the truth. Radiance is still the first Riftbound set with newly commissioned Showcase artwork rather than reused Legends of Runeterra art, and those arts span several League skinlines — K/DA, Heartsteel and Pool Party all appeared across our boxes.

Why do I keep pulling so many Legends in Radiance?

Radiance has nine Legend champion cards — Seraphine, Evelynn, Ekko, Ziggs, Jarvan IV, Orianna, K'Sante, Mordekaiser and Riven — sitting at Rare and filling the rare-or-above slots. That said, the display we tallied produced about 8 Legends across 24 packs, noticeably fewer than the 12 per box that Vendetta produced and that all six boxes of the Unleashed sealed case produced. The count is still single-box, but the breadth is confirmed across the sample: every display that named its cards turned up all nine Legends. If the lower count holds up over more boxes, the Legend-density complaint that dogged the last two sets is genuinely improving.

How many alt-art Runes are in a Radiance booster box?

Roughly one per box, but often none. Across our seven displays the count was 1, 1, 0, 0, 1, 2, 1 — six in 168 packs, about 3.5% of packs or 0.86 per display. This is the least settled number on the page, and we have now moved it twice: published at one per box off two displays, cut to one per two boxes when the next two produced none, raised again when a sixth display produced two. With only six alt-art Runes in the cycle (R01A–R06A) the counts swing hard, so read it as "roughly one, often none" rather than a decimal. They are not in the Vault — the Vault's 36 Runes are the basic set. Because a deck can run up to eight copies of a single Rune, these carry genuine player demand on top of collector demand, which still makes them the tier most likely to be underpriced in launch week.

Is the Radiance Vault Bundle a better way to open packs?

Not on a per-pack basis — it is six packs. We now have three Vaults in the sample and they ran wildly differently: one produced an Overnumbered Orianna plus Orianna's signature spell, one produced a K'Sante Showcase, and one produced a single Epic and nothing else (its owner called it "giga low rolled"). Six packs is far too few to expect anything. What the Vault is actually for is the complete 36-Rune basic set, plus a storage box and full-art foil gold and Bomb tokens. If you are new to Riftbound, that Rune set is the cheapest route to a playable collection. If you already have Runes from Origins, Unleashed or Vendetta, you are paying a bundle premium for six packs. Note the Vault contains the basic Runes — the music-themed alt-art Runes only come from packs. Full product breakdown in our Radiance products guide.

Did anyone actually pull the Packed Amphitheater Ultimate?

Yes. One creator pulled it from a Radiance Summit booster display on October 19, four days before retail launch — the first confirmed Packed Amphitheater Ultimate anywhere. We are reporting it prominently and refusing to treat it as rate evidence, which deserves an explanation.

That same 24-pack display also produced two Overnumbered, at a point when the six other displays in our sample had produced none at all between them across 144 packs. And partway through the box, before the second Overnumbered appeared, its opener says on camera: "Riot knows I bring the people to Riftbound. They're like, yo, we're going to juice your box, right?" He meant it as a joke. The box then kept delivering.

At Riot's stated 0.025%, the expected number of Ultimates in 312 display packs is 0.08. One hit in 330 packs implies a rate of about 0.30% per pack — roughly 12 times the published figure — off a single observation, in the one box we have specific reason to distrust, which is also the box that broke the Overnumbered pattern. A sealed case opened three days later added none. Between a coincidence and a seeded creator box, the number Riot published for the actual print run is the better estimate for anyone buying off a shelf. So the rate on this page and in our calculator stays at 1 in 4,000 packs. Full reasoning here.

What comes in a Riftbound Radiance Pre-Rift kit, and is it worth it?

A retail Pre-Rift event kit runs about $35 and contains a seeded preconstructed booster built around one of the nine Legends (fixed contents, not a random pack), five ordinary booster packs, and the Heimerdinger, Academy Instructor promo stamped as a pre-release card.

On a per-pack basis it is poor value, and one opener did the arithmetic out loud: $35 for five packs plus a fixed booster is dearer per pack than a $120 display, and he said he would not recommend it to anyone who just wants to rip packs. What a kit is actually for is the event itself and that Heimerdinger promo, which is not obtainable any other way. Of the two kits we have data for, one produced a Jarvan IV Overnumbered and the other produced nothing above Epic. See the retail section for why those are reported but not pooled into our rates, and our Radiance products guide for the full SKU breakdown.

How does Radiance compare to Vendetta for pull rates?

The structure is identical — 24 packs per display, three foils per pack, two rare-or-above slots, $120 MSRP. At 330 packs the two sets have turned out nearly the same. Epics land at 6–8 per box against Vendetta's 6–7, and alt-art Showcase Epics at 2.3 per box against Vendetta's 2–3 total alt arts. Radiance may carry fewer Legends — about 8 against 12 — but that rests on one display. Early Radiance data looked much hotter than any of this; the gap closed as the sample grew. Where the sets genuinely differ is the chase: Vendetta's Crystal Rose (Wild Rift) is replaced by the Packed Amphitheater Ultimate at a Riot-stated 1 in 4,000 packs, and Radiance Overnumbered now measures ~0.29 per display against Vendetta's roughly one per 2–3 boxes — eleven of our thirteen displays produced none, and a whole sealed case produced one. See our Vendetta box data for the comparison baseline.
